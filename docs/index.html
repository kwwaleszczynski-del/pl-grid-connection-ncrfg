Grid connection studies and non-firm capacity on Polish MV/110 kV feeders

Steady-state and N-1 grid connection study of a Polish 110/15 kV substation (GPZ, two parallel 25 MVA OLTC transformers) and four radial 15 kV feeders: how conductor R/X ratio, NC RfG Type B/C autonomous Q(U) and cos-phi(P) reactive power control, non-firm active power export caps, and co-located BESS cable pooling govern connectable renewable capacity. Code and method: github.com/kwwaleszczynski-del/pl-grid-connection-ncrfg.

1. Interactive feeder connection & NC RfG simulator

Select a 15 kV feeder, an NC RfG reactive / active power control regime, and candidate installed PV capacity (2 MW to 20 MW). The left chart shows the exact Newton-Raphson AC voltage profile along the feeder against the +5.0 % Polish DSO PCC rise ceiling (1.050 pu); the right chart shows the 7-day sunny May time-series scaled to the selected capacity.

2. Hosting capacity across Feeders F1-F3 and GPZ N-1

Maximum connectable capacity (MW) satisfying both steady-state PCC voltage rise (U ≤ 1.050 pu), continuous conductor ampacity (I ≤ I_dd), and 110/15 kV transformer N-1 backfeed (S ≤ 25.0 MVA).

3. Non-firm export cap vs curtailed energy and GPZ N-1 transformer loading

Capping peak active export to 70 % of installed MW sacrifices 2.4 % of annual PV generation while unlocking +43 % more connectable capacity on both voltage-limited rural feeders and N-1 transformer-limited substations. Co-located 2 h BESS cable pooling absorbs midday peaks and cuts spilled energy below 0.6 %.

4. Solver verification against exact analytical 2-bus solution

The polar Newton-Raphson Ybus solver is checked against the closed-form biquadratic 2-bus AC power flow solution and exact nodal power conservation.

Standards & grid code reference: Commission Regulation (EU) 2016/631 (NC RfG) Type B/C reactive power capability, PN-EN 50160 voltage quality, and Polish DSO (IRiESD / PTPiREE) medium-voltage connection evaluation practice. Code: MIT licence.
